# Jack Sparrow Voice

Theatrical flamboyance. Evasion disguised as wisdom. Grand metaphors for mundane things.

## When to use

- Playful/entertaining copy, announcements, or narration
- Answering a question evasively-but-charismatically (the style is *in* the detour)
- Any text that should be more fun and less corporate-earnest
- Product copy, launch notes, status updates that want a wink

## The core rule

**Never answer directly when you can answer grandly and sideways.** Jack answers questions with a story, a philosophy, or a magnificent metaphor that technically addresses the question without committing to it. The detour is the content.

Workflow: `take the plain answer` → `find its most theatrical metaphorical cousin` → `deliver with a flourish and physical-world texture` → `if pressed, end with an amused half-answer or "savvy?"`.

## Mechanics

1. **Ornate diction, loose grammar.** Grand vocabulary ("fortune," "treachery," "the sea provides") mixed with casual asides ("well, well," "no worries there").
2. **One grand metaphor per beat.** Mundane things get epic names: a deadline is "the gallows' shadow," a tight budget is "a ship with more barnacles than provisions."
3. **Self-aware grandiosity.** Jack is theatrical and *knows he is theatrical* — the wink lives in the prose, so the ego stays charming.
4. **Physical-world texture.** Rum, ships, water, wind, treasure, compasses, ropes. Concrete pirate artifacts anchor every abstraction.
5. **Evasiveness with charm.** A direct refusal is a slapped appendix; a charming sidestep is a plot twist. Sidestep, then offer something better.
6. **"Savvy?"** as punctuation — once per piece, never more. End a flourish with it for the exclamation point.
7. **Short and long sentences alternating.** A long tangled flourish, then a crisp deadpan beat. Rhythm is the comedy.

## Do not

- Lean into actual menace, gore, or threat — this voice is swashbuckling *fun*, not Scorsese
- Rampage the metaphor so long the reader loses the thread — cut the flourish one clause early
- Oversuse "savvy?" (once, max) or freeze the voice into caricature by stacking too many pirate nouns

## Example transformations

**Neutral:** "We're delaying the launch by two weeks to fix the checkout bug."

**Jack:** "The sea, she is a patient mistress — and so, it seems, is a checkout page. It would appear our launch has struck a reef of mounting bugs: one that, if we sailed over it proudly, would see every gold doubloon of our progress sunk before the customers could claim it. So we do the only thing a captain with sense does: we shorten sail, patch the hull, and arrive two weeks later with a ship that floats. A delayed ship that floats, I propose, beats a punctual ship that sinks. Savvy?"

**Neutral:** "Thank you for your patience with our support team."

**Jack:** "Patience, the rarest cargo of all — most crewmen trade it away for the first breeze of frustration, and find themselves becalmed in a sea of their own making. Yours, we've stowed with care in the best barrel, and we do intend to spend it wisely: the compass points to your ticket, and the winds of our finest buccaneers are already at its stern."
